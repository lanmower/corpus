### Quadrilaterals — Definitions, Properties and Areas

A **quadrilateral** is any four-sided figure. The interior angles of *any* quadrilateral add up to **360°** (split it with one diagonal into two triangles: 2 × 180° = 360°).

The special quadrilaterals lie on a "pathway": as you move from a general quadrilateral towards the **square**, properties **accumulate** — each shape keeps everything the earlier ones have and adds its own. The square is the "ultimate" quadrilateral: it is a rectangle *and* a rhombus (and therefore a parallelogram and a kite), so **all** their properties apply to it.

**The shapes (definition · properties · area):**

- **Trapezium** — one pair of opposite sides parallel. Area = ½(a + b)·h, where a and b are the two parallel sides and h the perpendicular distance between them ("half the sum of the parallel sides × the distance between them").
- **Parallelogram** — two pairs of opposite sides parallel. Properties: opposite sides equal; opposite angles equal; **diagonals bisect one another** (but are not equal and not perpendicular in general). Area = base × height (perpendicular height).
- **Rectangle** — a parallelogram with one right angle (so all four angles are 90°). Extra property: **diagonals are equal** (and still bisect each other). Area = ℓ × b.
- **Rhombus** — a parallelogram with one pair of adjacent sides equal (so **all four sides equal**). Diagonals **bisect each other perpendicularly**, bisect the angles, and bisect the area. Area = ½ × product of diagonals (as for a kite) **or** base × height (as for a parallelogram).
- **Kite** — two pairs of *adjacent* sides equal. Diagonals cut perpendicularly, and **one** diagonal bisects the other diagonal, the opposite angles and the area. Area = ½ × product of the diagonals ("half the product of the diagonals").
- **Square** — the rectangle-and-rhombus combination: all sides equal, all angles 90°, diagonals equal, perpendicular, bisecting each other and the angles. Area = s².

**Quick diagonal reference:**
- Equal diagonals: rectangle, square.
- Perpendicular diagonals: rhombus, kite, square.
- Diagonals bisect each other: parallelogram, rectangle, rhombus, square (in a kite, only one does).

**Worked example:** A rhombus has diagonals 6 cm and 8 cm. Area = ½ × 6 × 8 = 24 cm². (The same ½ × d₁ × d₂ formula works for a kite.)

**Note on the perpendicular diagonals of a rhombus:** at the centre the half-angles satisfy 2x + 2y = 180° (angles of a triangle, or co-interior angles between the parallel sides), so dividing by 2 gives x + y = 90° — confirming the diagonals meet at a right angle.

**Pitfalls:** use the *perpendicular* height for parallelogram/rhombus area, not the slanted side; remember that in a general parallelogram the diagonals are **not** equal; and that a kite has only **one** diagonal that bisects the other.
