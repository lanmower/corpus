### Geometric Constructions

A construction is a drawing made with only a **compass** and an **unmarked straight-edge** — no protractor for angles and no reading of ruler markings to set lengths (except to lay off a given side). Marks are always given to the construction arcs that reveal your method, so keep all arcs light and visible; do not erase them.

**Perpendicular bisector of a line segment AB.** Open the compass wider than half of AB. From A draw arcs above and below the line; with the same radius from B draw arcs that cut them, at P and Q. The line PQ cuts AB at its midpoint and at 90°. Every point on PQ is the same distance from A and B. If the radius is not wider than half of AB the arcs never meet.

**Bisecting an angle ABC.** From the vertex B draw an arc cutting both arms at D and E. From D and from E (same radius) draw arcs that cross at F. Ray BF bisects the angle, so ∠ABF = ∠FBC.

**Perpendiculars.** *At a point P on the line:* draw an arc from P cutting the line at A and B, then construct the perpendicular bisector of AB. *From a point P off the line:* draw an arc from P cutting the line at A and B, then from A and B draw arcs crossing on the far side at Q; PQ is perpendicular.

**Parallel line through P.** Draw a transversal through P meeting the line at A, then copy the angle at A to P (using the compass to reproduce the arc width). Equal corresponding angles force the lines to be parallel.

**Special angles.** A **60°** angle comes from the equilateral-triangle method: from A draw an arc cutting the line at C; with the same radius from C cut the arc at D; AD makes 60°. **30°** is a bisected 60°; **90°** is a perpendicular at a point; **45°** is a bisected 90°; **15°** is a bisected 30°.

**Triangles and quadrilaterals.** Given three sides (SSS), draw the longest side, then swing two arcs of the other lengths to locate the third vertex — possible only if the two shorter sides add to more than the longest (triangle inequality). Given two sides and the included angle (SAS), draw one side, construct the angle, measure the second side along the new arm. A quadrilateral is built by splitting it along a diagonal into two triangles and constructing each in turn; a rectangle uses two 90° angles and the compass to set the equal sides.
