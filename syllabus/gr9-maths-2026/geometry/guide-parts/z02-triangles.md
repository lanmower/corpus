### Triangles — Classification and Angle Properties

Triangles are classified two ways. **By sides:** *equilateral* (all three sides equal, hence all three angles 60°), *isosceles* (two equal sides, with the two base angles opposite them equal), and *scalene* (all sides — and angles — different). **By angles:** *acute* (all angles below 90°), *right-angled* (one 90° angle, with the hypotenuse opposite it as the longest side), and *obtuse* (one angle above 90°). A triangle can hold at most one right or obtuse angle.

**Angle sum.** The three interior angles of any triangle add to 180°. The acceptable reason is "∠ sum of △". To find a missing angle, subtract the known angles from 180°; for angles given in terms of x, add them, set the sum equal to 180°, and solve. For example 3x + 2x + x = 180° gives x = 30°.

**Exterior-angle theorem.** An exterior angle (formed by extending one side) equals the **sum of the two opposite interior angles**; the reason is "ext ∠ of △". This follows because the exterior angle plus its adjacent interior angle make 180° (∠s on a straight line), and the three interior angles also make 180°. So an exterior angle of 125° with one opposite interior angle of 70° gives the other as 125° − 70° = 55°. An exterior angle is always larger than either opposite interior angle.

**Isosceles work.** Identify which side is the odd one out; the equal angles lie *opposite the equal sides* (reason: "∠s opp equal sides"). If a base angle is 65°, the other base angle is also 65° and the apex is 180° − 65° − 65° = 50°. If the apex is given, set each base angle to x and solve 2x + apex = 180°.
