### Euclidean Geometry: Theorem Statements, Acceptable Reasons & Pythagoras

In Grade 9 geometry every statement in a proof must be justified with an **acceptable reason** — the exact short wording the marker expects. Learn the statement AND its reason word-for-word; the abbreviation is worth the mark.

#### Lines and angles
- Adjacent angles on a straight line are supplementary (add to 180°) — **∠s on a str line**.
- If adjacent angles are supplementary, the outer arms form a straight line — **adj ∠s supp**.
- Angles in a revolution add to 360° — **∠s round a pt** OR **∠s in a rev**.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal — **vert opp ∠s**.

#### Parallel lines (always name the parallel lines)
- AB ∥ CD ⇒ alternate angles equal — **alt ∠s; AB ∥ CD**.
- AB ∥ CD ⇒ corresponding angles equal — **corresp ∠s; AB ∥ CD**.
- AB ∥ CD ⇒ co-interior angles supplementary — **co-int ∠s; AB ∥ CD**.
- Converses (to prove lines parallel): **alt ∠s =**, **corresp ∠s =**, **co-int ∠s supp**.

#### Triangles
- Interior angles add to 180° — **∠ sum in △** / **sum of ∠s in △** / **int ∠s in △**.
- Exterior angle = sum of two interior opposite angles — **ext ∠ of △**.
- Isosceles: angles opposite equal sides are equal — **∠s opp equal sides**; converse: sides opposite equal angles are equal — **sides opp equal ∠s**.
- Midpoint theorem: segment joining midpoints of two sides is parallel to the third side and half its length — **Midpt Theorem**; converse: line from a midpoint parallel to another side bisects the third side — **line through midpt ∥ to 2ⁿᵈ side**.
- Proportion: a line parallel to one side divides the other two proportionally — **line ∥ one side of △** / **prop theorem; name ∥ lines**; converse — **line divides two sides of △ in prop**.
- Similarity: equiangular triangles have sides in proportion — **||| △s** / **equiangular △s**; converse — **Sides of △ in prop**.
- Equal areas: triangles/parallelograms on the same base (or equal bases) between the same parallels have equal areas — **same base; same height** / **equal bases; equal height**.

#### Congruency conditions
- **SSS** (three sides), **SAS** / **S∠S** (two sides + included angle), **AAS** / **∠∠S** (two angles + corresponding side), **RHS** / **90°HS** (right angle, hypotenuse, side).

#### Quadrilaterals
- Interior angles add to 360° — **sum of ∠s in quad**.
- Parallelogram: opposite sides parallel and equal — **opp sides of ∥m**; opposite angles equal — **opp ∠s of ∥m**; diagonals bisect each other — **diag of ∥m**; a diagonal bisects its area — **diag bisect area of ∥m**. Converses to prove a parallelogram use phrases like **opp sides of quad are ∥**, **opp sides of quad are =**, **opp ∠s of quad are =**, **diags of quad bisect each other**, **pair of opp sides = and ∥**.
- Rhombus: diagonals bisect each other at right angles and bisect the interior angles, all sides equal — **diags of rhombus** / **sides of rhombus**.
- Square: all sides equal — **sides of square**.
- Rectangle: diagonals equal — **diags of rect**.
- Kite: diagonals intersect at right angles, one diagonal bisects the other and bisects the opposite angles — **diags of kite**.

#### Theorem of Pythagoras
In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides: c² = a² + b², where c (the hypotenuse) is opposite the right angle — reason **Pythagoras** / **Theorem of Pythagoras**.

**Worked example:** legs 6 cm and 8 cm. hyp² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100, so hyp = 10 cm.

**Converse of Pythagoras:** if the square of the longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two, the triangle is right-angled — reason **Converse Pythagoras** / **Converse Theorem of Pythagoras**. Use it to PROVE a right angle.

**Worked example:** sides 5, 12, 13. Since 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13², the triangle is right-angled (right angle opposite the 13 side).

**Pitfall:** always quote the reason exactly and, for parallel-line reasons, name the parallel lines (e.g. "alt ∠s; AB ∥ CD"). Marks are lost for vague or missing reasons.
