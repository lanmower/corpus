### Simple Interest

**Simple interest** is calculated on the original principal only, the same amount every year. It applies to many short-term loans and investments.

**Formulas**
- I = P × r × t (also written I = P·i·n)
- A = P + I = P(1 + i·n)
- Solve for P: P = I ÷ (r·t)
- Solve for r: r = I ÷ (P·t)
- Solve for t: t = I ÷ (P·r)

Here P = principal, r or i = annual rate as a **decimal**, t or n = time in **years**, I = interest, A = final amount. Convert a rate by dividing by 100 (7,5% = 0,075) and convert months to years by dividing by 12 (18 months = 1,5 years).

**Worked example 1.** R5 000 at 8% simple interest for 3 years.
I = 5 000 × 0,08 × 3 = R1 200. A = 5 000 + 1 200 = R6 200. (Check: 5 000 × 1,24 = R6 200.)

**Worked example 2 (solve for r).** R4 000 earns R720 over 4 years.
r = 720 ÷ (4 000 × 4) = 720 ÷ 16 000 = 0,045 = 4,5% per year.

For a **loan**, the interest is what you pay extra; for an **investment**, it is what you earn — the formula is identical.

**Pitfalls**
- Using the rate as a whole number (8 instead of 0,08) inflates interest 100×.
- Leaving time in months instead of converting to years.
- Forgetting to convert the rate decimal back to a percentage in the final answer when solving for r.
